# Setting Up pfSense On A Protectli Vault

When I first wanted to learn networking, I picked up a firewall/router from Netgate:  the [SG-1100](https://shop.netgate.com/products/1100-pfsense). Netgate is the company behind pfSense, and for what it is, the SG-1100 is an impressive little machine. However, it does have limitations. VPN throughput tops out at roughly 80 Mbps, and the modest CPU struggles when running heavier packages like pfBlockerNG or Snort.

I eventually decided to look for a more capable solution that didn’t cost $600 or more. That search led me to the Protectli Vault [FW4C](https://protectli.com/inteltechniques/).  The FW4C ships with:

- Intel Quad-Core J3710
- 4 GB DDR3 RAM
- 32 GB mSATA SSD
- coreboot (open-source BIOS)

For a home or small-lab firewall, these specs are more than sufficient.

## What You’ll Need

- A Protectli Vault (2-, 4-, or 6-port model)

> The 2-port model requires a managed switch for wireless access. This guide uses the 4-port model.
{: .prompt-info }

- A computer or laptop (I’m using Pop!_OS)
- An active internet connection
- A Proton VPN subscription

## Download and Install pfSense

1. Navigate to the pfSense [download](https://atxfiles.netgate.com/mirror/downloads/) page.
2. Choose **pfSense-CE-memstick-2.7.2-RELEASE-amd64.img.gz**.
3. Download the `.gz` file and extract it.
4. Confirm you now have an `.img` file (for example, `pfSense-CE-2.7.2-RELEASE-amd64.img`).
5. [Download](https://etcher.balena.io) and install **balenaEtcher**.
6. Open balenaEtcher and click **Flash from file**.
7. Select the pfSense `.img` file.
8. Click **Select target** and choose your USB flash drive.
9. Click **Flash** and wait for the process to complete.

> This will erase the USB drive. Make sure it contains no important data.
{: .prompt-danger }

## Install pfSense on the Protectli Vault

1. Ensure the Protectli Vault is powered off.
2. Connect a monitor and USB keyboard directly to the device.
3. Insert the USB flash drive.
4. Power on the Vault and confirm it boots into the installer.

> The bottom USB port on Protectli devices tends to work best for installations.
{: .prompt-tip }

When the installer appears, press **Enter** to begin. Accept all default options. During the ZFS configuration step, select the internal drive by highlighting it and pressing the space bar. Confirm the installation when prompted.

When installation completes:

- Choose **No** when asked to open a shell
- Select **Reboot**

After the reboot completes and you hear the startup tone, press the power button once to shut the system down. Remove the USB drive, monitor, and keyboard. The firewall is now ready for configuration.

## Initial Login and Setup

1. Connect your computer to the **LAN** port on the Protectli Vault.
2. Connect your modem or upstream router to the **WAN** port.
3. Power on the Vault and wait for the startup beep.
4. Ensure your computer is disconnected from all other networks (Wi-Fi included).
5. Navigate to `https://192.168.1.1` in a browser.

Ignore the certificate warning and proceed. Log in using:

- **Username:** `admin`
- **Password:** `pfsense`

Click **Next** through the setup wizard, accepting defaults. If you want to change the LAN IP address, do it here (I used 172.16.17.1). Set a strong admin password when prompted. Continue until you reach the dashboard, accept the trademark notice, and close the wizard.

## Activate and Assign Additional Interfaces

### Assign and configure OPT1 and OPT2:

- Navigate to **Interface** > **Assignments** and add any pending ports then **Save**

### OPT1 Configuration

- Navigate to **Interfaces** > **OPT1** 
- Enable the interface
- IPv4 Configuration Type: **Static IPv4**
- IPv4 Address: `172.16.34.1/24`
- Click **Save**; then **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **Firewall** then **Rules** and click **OPT1**
- Click **Add** (up arrow) and change the Protocol to **Any**
- Change source to **OPT1 subnets**
- Click **Display Advanced** and change the Gateway to **Wan_DHCP…**
- Click **Save** and **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **Services** > **DHCP Server**
- Enable DHCP with a range of `172.16.34.10 – 172.16.34.245`
- Click **Save** then **Apply Changes**

### OPT2 Configuration

- Navigate to **Interfaces** > **OPT2**
- Enable the interface
- IPv4 Configuration Type: **Static IPv4**
- IPv4 Address: `172.16.51.1/24`
- Click **Save**; then **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **Firewall** then **Rules** and click **OPT2**
- Click **Add** (up arrow) and change the Protocol to **Any**
- Change source to **OPT2 subnets**
- Click **Display Advanced** and change the Gateway to **Wan_DHCP…**
- Click **Save** and **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **Services** > **DHCP Server**
- Enable DHCP with a range of `172.16.51.10 – 172.16.51.245`
- Click **Save** then **Apply Changes**

## System Optimization

- Navigate to **System** > **Advanced** > **Networking**.
- Select **KEA DHCP** and click **Save**.
- Navigate to **System** > **Advanced** > **Miscellaneous**.
- Enable **PowerD** and ensure **Hiadaptive** is chosen for each option.
- Scroll to the Cryptographic & Thermal Hardware section.
- Select **AES-NI CPU-based Acceleration** in the first drop-down menu.
- Select **Intel...** from the Thermal Sensors menu, click **Save**.
- Navigate to **System** > **Advanced** > **Notifications**.
- In the E-mail section, enable the **Disable SMTP** option, click **Save**.

## Configure WireGuard with Proton VPN

- Log into your [Proton](https://account.protonvpn.com/downloads) VPN account
- In the **Wireguard** section, enter a name of **pfsense** for a new connection
- Choose **Router** for the **Select platform**
- Select your desired server and click **Create**
- Download the file created.
- Navigate to **System** > **Package Manager** > **Available Packages**
- Search WireGuard, click **Install** next to WireGuard, then **Confirm**
- Allow the installation to complete
- Navigate to **VPN** > **WireGuard** and click the **+Add Tunnel** button
- Apply a Description of **ProtonTunnel** and Listen Port of **51820**
- Copy the PrivateKey data, which is presented in the Proton file which you downloaded, and paste it into the **Interface Keys** field
- Click the **Public key** field and allow the public key to be generated
- Click **Save Tunnel** then click **Peers** in the top menu
- Click the **+Add Peer** button
- Change the Tunnel to the **ProtonTunnel** option previously created
- Apply a Description of **ProtonPeer**
- Disable the option next to **Dynamic Endpoint**
- Enter the Endpoint address and port from your file previously downloaded
- Enter a value of **25** within Keep Alive
- Copy the PublicKey data, which is presented in the Proton file which you downloaded, and paste it into the **Public Key** field
- Enter **0.0.0.0** within Allowed IPs and change 128 to **0**
- Click **Save Peer** then click **Settings** in the upper menu
- Enable **Enable WireGuard** and click **Save** then **Apply Changes**
- Click **Status** in the upper menu and ensure a green Up connection
- Select **Interfaces** and click **Assignments**
- Next to **tun_wg0** at the bottom, click **Add** then **Save**
- Click the new option at bottom **OPT3**
- Enable **Enable Interface** and provide a Description of **ProtonInterface**
- Change IPv4 Configuration Type to **Static IPv4**
- Enter **10.2.0.2** into IPv4 Address and click **Save** and **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **System** > **Routing** and click **Add**
- Change the Interface to **ProtonInterface**
- Change the Name to **ProtonGateway**
- Change the Gateway to **10.2.0.1**
- Enable **Disable Gateway Monitoring** **Action**
- Select **Kill states using this gateway....**
- Click **Display Advanced** and check **Use non-local gateway**
- Click **Save** and **Apply Changes**
- As long as we are on this page click the pencil icon under **WAN_DHCP6**, check **Disable this gateway**
- Click **Save** and **Apply Changes**
- Navigate to **Interfaces** > **ProtonInterface**
- Change the IPv4 Upstream Gateway to **ProtonGateway**
- Enable **Block private networks** **and** **loopback addresses**
- Enable **Block Bogon Networks** and **Save** then **Apply changes**
- Navigate to **System** > **Advanced** > **Miscellaneous**
- Change State Killing on Gateway Failure to **Kill states for all gateways...**
- Enable the option next to **Skip rules when gateway is down**
- Click **Save**

Confirm the tunnel shows **Up** under WireGuard status.

## Route OPT2 Through the VPN

- Navigate to **Firewall** > **NAT** > **Outbound**.
- Select **Manual Outbound NAT rule generation**.
- Click **Save** then **Apply Changes**.
- Click the checkbox for every entry which has **ProtonInterface** as the Interface, click  **delete**, confirm by clicking **OK**.
- Click **Apply Changes**.
- Click the **pencil icon** next to the option with a description similar to **Auto created rule - OPT2 to WAN**.
- Change the Interface option of WAN to **ProtonInterface**.
- Change the Address Family to **IPv4** and click **Save**.
- Click the **pencil icon** next to the option with a description similar to **Auto created rule for ISAKMP - OPT2 to WAN**.
- Change the Interface option of WAN to **ProtonInterface**.
- Click **Save** then **Apply Changes**.
- Navigate to **Firewall** > **Rules** > **OPT2**.
- Click the **pencil icon** to edit the setting.
- If required, click the **Display Advanced button**.
- Change the Gateway to **ProtonGateway**.
- Click **Save**.
- Click the **Disable icon** next to **Default allow LAN IPv6 to any rule**.
- Click **Apply Changes**.

## Configure DNS (NextDNS via Proton)

- Navigate to **System** > **General Setup**.
- Add **45.90.28.232** as the first DNS server.
- Enter **dns.nextdns.io** as the first DNS Hostname.
- Select **WAN**.
- Click **Add DNS Server**.
- Add **45.90.30.232** as the second DNS server.
- Enter **dns.nextdns.io** as the second DNS Hostname.
- Select **ProtonGateway**.
- Disable **DNS server override**.
- Change DNS Resolution Behavior to **Use local DNS (127.0.0.1), ignore remote DNS** and click **Save**.
- Navigate to **Services** > **DNS Resolver**.
- Within Outgoing Network Interfaces, select **ProtonInterface**.
- Enable **Strict Outgoing Network Interface Binding**.
- Enable **DNSSEC**.
- Enable **DNS Query Forwarding**.
- Enable **Use SSL/TLS for outgoing DNS Queries to Forwarding Servers**.
- Click **Save** then **Apply Changes**.

## Configure Home Screen

- Navigate to the home screen and click the **x** by **Netgate Services And Support**.
- Add **WireGuard**, **Gateways**, **Thermal**, **Packages**, and **S.M.A.R.T.**.

## Disable Logging (once everything is stable)

- Navigate to **Status** > **System Logs** > **Settings**.
- Click **Reset** button.
- Enable **Disable writing log**, read the warning .
- Disable all Logging Preferences.

## Test and Backup

- Open a new web browser tab and type in **what is my IP**.
- If on OPT2 the IP should be the VPN IP.
- Navigate to **Diagnostics** > **Backup & Restore**.
- Click **Download configuration as XML**.

## Change VPN Servers (when needed)

- Navigate to **VPN** > **Wireguard** > **Edit Tunnel**.
- Replace Interface Key with **Private Key** from new file.
- Click out side of Private Key box and allow new Public Key to generate.
- Click **Save Tunnel** then **Peers** > **Edit Peer**.
- Replace **Endpoint** with the IP address in the file.
- Replace **Public Key** with the Public Key from the file.
- Click **Save Peer** > **Apply Changes**.
- **Stop** and **Restart** Wireguard.
- Open a new web browser tab and type in **what is my IP**.

## Final Thoughts

That’s it, you now have a fast, privacy-focused pfSense router. In my setup, LAN handles wired devices, OPT1 serves Wi-Fi that must bypass VPNs (like streaming services), and OPT2 routes traffic exclusively through Proton VPN.

With the Protectli Vault, I get full VPN speeds while running multiple pfSense packages without performance issues.
